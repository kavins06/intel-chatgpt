# Historical case lessons

**Public-edition access note:** S01-S28 are bibliography references; their original PDFs and extracted text are not included. Page pointers help locate an authorized original copy. Verify the actual passage before citing it as evidence.

Use analogies to generate hypotheses and tests. A past outcome does not establish that a new situation has the same mechanism. Separate the reading's account from intel's operational inference and verify case details before quoting.

## Lehman: visibility, incentives, and exposure

Sources: S01, S16, S18. Start with S16 PDF pp. 44-65 for the introduction and executive summary, then search its risk section for the exact issue. S18 discusses financial reporting and Repo 105; S01 is a brief video transcript with simplified leverage language.

Investigate what management measured, whether limits constrained decisions, how funding and asset liquidity interacted, and whether reported snapshots represented continuing exposure. Compare ratios only after reconciling dates and definitions. Study incentives, governance, exception handling, and challenge channels alongside market conditions. Distinguish liquidity from solvency and reporting choices from the underlying economics.

Limit: S16 is Volume 1 of 9. Its introduction and executive summary do contain substantive Repo 105 findings (PDF pp. 49-50 and 60-64), while its contents point to the detailed investigation in absent Volume 3. Use those present primary summaries for what they establish; use S18 explicitly as a secondary account and obtain the relevant original volume when detailed transaction evidence is required. An examiner's conclusions about colorable claims are not a court's final adjudication. Read PDF pp. 95-96 when assessing the difference between risk-control failures and the examiner's legal conclusions. S01's historical leverage figures need definition and corroboration, not automatic reconciliation by averaging.

## Afghanistan: competing accounts and decision timing

Sources: S04, S12, S13. Compare the official retrospective with reporting on the State Department review and testimony. Record who said what, when, with what responsibility and incentives. Seek the underlying review or hearing for consequential claims; reporting of it remains secondary unless the original is inspected.

Separate long-term objectives, negotiated constraints, force levels, intelligence about capabilities and will, evacuation timing, interagency coordination, and execution. Reconstruct what was known at each point rather than using the eventual outcome as proof of foreseeability. Model alternative explanations and distinguish policy decisions from collection or analysis failures.

Limit: disagreement among institutional accounts does not establish that one is wholly false or that responsibility must be divided equally. Do not infer operational capability from equipment or personnel counts alone.

## Blockbuster: adaptation requires timing and execution

Source: S26, an MSc dissertation and teaching case from 2011. Start with the abstract, table of contents, case narrative, discussion, and conclusion; trace cited original records for critical empirical claims.

Analyze changes in customer behavior, distribution, business-model incentives, resources, leadership perceptions, financing, and the timing and quality of responses. Separate awareness of a threat from the ability and incentive to act. Compare feasible alternatives at the time.

Limit: a teaching case illustrates a theoretical interpretation; it is not a controlled causal study. Do not reduce the outcome to 'failed to see streaming' or infer that one rejected opportunity alone caused failure without evidence.

## Cambridge Analytica: access is not authorization

Source: S15, a supplied capture of 2018 investigative reporting. Identify consent, collection scope, onward use, assurances, oversight, and accountability. In today's work verify data origin, permitted use, and collection authority separately from technical availability.

Limit: historical reporting and allegations require correct attribution; the capture includes web layout interruptions. Do not assume it contains the complete article or that reported data access proves claimed persuasion effectiveness. Apply the case to privacy and governance, not to instructions for covert targeting.

## INR: dissent needs evidence and a route to decisions

Source: S23, a 2024 journalistic profile. Explore subject expertise, institutional memory, evidence-based dissent, and whether unwelcome judgments reach decision makers. Maintain a strong competing hypothesis rather than defaulting to consensus.

Limit: a selected profile of successes is not an audited accuracy dataset. Do not repeat 'almost always right' as an established measured fact. Contrarianism alone is not analytical quality.

## Everybody Matters: human effects and information quality

Source: S19, an undated documentary transcript. Examine trust, listening, employee experience, incentives, and whether people can surface problems. Use inclusive engagement to understand adoption and implementation risks.

Limit: anecdotes and reported workplace statistics require the original study before numerical or causal claims are relied upon. Transcript speaker attribution and date are incomplete.
