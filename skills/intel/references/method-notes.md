# Method notes and adaptations

**Public-edition access note:** S01-S28 are bibliography references; their original PDFs and extracted text are not included. Page pointers help locate an authorized original copy. Verify the actual passage before citing it as evidence.

These notes operationalize the supplied readings. They are a synthesis, not quotations. Competing-hypothesis tests, forecast scoring, reversible experiments, and structured record validation are intel extensions and should not be attributed to a source that does not teach them.

| Method | Best use | Minimum working output | Common failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intelligence cycle (S21-S22) | Recurring decision support | Question, collection, analysis, communication, action, feedback | Collecting before defining a use; confusing information with intelligence |
| Issue tree / MECE (S25; introductory S10) | Structure a difficult question | Branches that answer the parent and leaf tests | Memorized framework; mixing dimensions; treating a tree as causal proof |
| Five forces (S20; applications S02/S05) | Industry economics | Defined industry and force-to-profit mechanisms | Listing competitors only; mixing industry and firm levels |
| PESTEL (S03/S17/S24) | External change | Material factor, causal channel, horizon, implication | Generic trend lists without an exposed decision |
| SWOT/TOWS (S14; examples S06/S07) | Synthesize internal and external evidence | Comparative factors translated into choices | Unsupported adjectives; treating internal capabilities as external opportunities |
| Stakeholder analysis (S09/S11) | Adoption, implementation, evaluation | Rights, interests, evidence of influence, inclusion, feedback | Title equals power; excluding affected low-power people |
| Blue Ocean (S28) | Redesign buyer value and economics | Value curve, ERRC, noncustomer hypothesis, utility-price-cost-adoption test | Novelty alone; unmeasured rating curves; demand assumed from market size |
| Innovation paths (S08; secondary S27) | Select type of market creation | Existing problem / reframed problem / new problem beyond boundaries | Calling every technology disruptive or nondisruptive without displacement analysis |
| Dynamic capabilities (S26) | Adaptation under change | Evidence of sensing, decisions, reconfiguration, and timing | One-cause decline narratives; hindsight and survivorship bias |
| Institutional dissent (S23) | Challenge consensus | Strong alternative with evidence and decision access | Treating contrarianism as accuracy or accepting profile superlatives as track record |

Choose methods by the question. PESTEL examines the broad environment; five forces examines competitive structure; capabilities examines the focal organization; SWOT summarizes; innovation redesigns; stakeholders addresses adoption; scenario analysis explores uncertainty. They answer different questions. A framework is a lens for evidence, not independent evidence itself.

For issue trees, use equations for quantities, steps for processes, segments for populations, and explicit conceptual distinctions for qualitative problems. Check arithmetic reconciliation and interactions. S10 does not include its linked Part 2; use S25's detailed discussion and original visual examples, and distinguish your adaptation from the supplied text.

For forecasts, specify a testable event before giving odds. For causal diagnosis, assess mechanisms and alternatives rather than recycling a persuasive story. For recommendations, compare expected benefit, cost, feasibility, downside, timing, and reversibility. Use explicit judgment when quantitative utility is unavailable.

For innovation terminology, distinguish the authors' later three-path taxonomy in S08 (PDF pp. 1, 3-4) from the broader use of blue oceans in the 2015 book (S28 PDF p. 27). The earlier book allows new market space beyond existing industry boundaries; do not force every historical use of Blue Ocean into an exclusive category. Use S28 PDF pp. 36-39 for value innovation and S08 for the later explicit comparison with nondisruptive creation.
